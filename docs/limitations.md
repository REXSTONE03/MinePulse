# MinePulse AI — Limitations, Downtime definitions, & Error Analysis

This document outlines the bounds of the predictive logic, defines downtime categories, and establishes the schema for prediction error diagnostics.

---

## 1. Rigorous Downtime Classifications

To prevent misleading results, the simulation engine distinguishes three distinct downtime types:

* **A. Mechanical Downtime**: The duration required to physically inspect and repair a vehicle (e.g. wrench time). 
* **B. Planned Maintenance Downtime**: The scheduled window during which a vehicle is stopped for routine, planned service tasks.
* **C. Parts-Unavailability Downtime (Primary KPI)**: The elapsed hours a vehicle remains stopped specifically because required parts are not in local warehouse inventory.

### Parts-Unavailability Downtime Event Boundaries
```
Failure/Maintenance event occurs
  |
  v
Identify required part & check inventory
  |
  +---> [In Stock] ----> Consume stock. Downtime = 0 hours.
  |
  +---> [Out of Stock] -> Vehicle enters Parts-Unavailability Downtime.
                         Place order.
                         Wait for supplier lead-time.
                         Part arrives at warehouse.
                         Downtime ends.
  |
  v
Proceed to mechanical repair (Mechanical Downtime starts)
```

## 2. Structured Error Analysis Schema

Every forecast deviation that leads to either a stockout or severe overstocking is logged in a structured diagnostic table in the database and visible in the UI:

| Column | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `prediction_qty` | REAL | Expected demand generated by the model. |
| `actual_qty` | INTEGER | Actual parts consumed. |
| `error_magnitude` | REAL | `actual_qty - prediction_qty` |
| `vehicle_id` | INTEGER | Impacted vehicle link. |
| `part_id` | INTEGER | Target part catalog link. |
| `forecast_date` | TEXT | Date prediction was generated. |
| `predicted_failure_risk`| REAL | Predicted probability of failure $P(Y_{v,c})$. |
| `error_classification`| TEXT | Diagnostic class (see below). |
| `probable_cause` | TEXT | Detailed rationalization. |

### Classification Categories
* `UNEXPECTED_FAILURE`: Failure occurred when $P(failure) < 0.10$.
* `SPARSE_DATA`: High error due to low training samples for rare components.
* `MAINTENANCE_RESCHEDULED`: Demand shifted due to calendar changes.
* `SUPPLIER_DISRUPTION`: Stockout prolonged because lead time exceeded catalog specs.
* `NEW_COMPONENT`: High prediction error due to lack of component-specific history.
* `MODEL_ERROR`: Standard statistical variance or classification error.

## 3. Modeling & Evaluation Limitations

1. **Synthetic Dataset Disclaimer**: The dataset is synthetically generated for controlled development, testing, temporal-leakage verification, and methodology demonstration. Results on synthetic data must NOT be presented as proof of real-world mining site performance.
2. **Weibull Baseline Comparison**: Out-of-sample backtesting demonstrated that while the Weibull competing-risks model captured 58.29% of failures at $P \ge 0.25$, it did NOT outperform the constant-hazard baseline on the overall Brier Score calibration metric (`0.1342` vs `0.0744`). This is due to heavy right-censoring caused by pre-emptive preventive maintenance replacements in the synthetic dataset. Further calibration and real-world site validation would be required prior to actual industrial deployment.
3. **Independent Failures Assumption**: Cascading wear is not modeled (e.g. an alternator failure leading to battery depletion).
4. **Stationary Daily Usage**: The model assumes average operating hours per day remain stable. Sudden shifts in shift patterns or operating intensity will cause forecasting lags.
5. **Deterministic Recommendation Lead Times**: The inventory optimization formula uses median lead times to calculate reorder points, even though the evaluation simulator models lead times as log-normal random variables.
